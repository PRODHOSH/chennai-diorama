Why we built this

A map shows where things are.
It doesn't show what happens there.

A normal map of Chennai will show you a railway station and a temple. It won't show you a grandmother drawing a kolam at five in the morning, a pot of Pongal boiling over, or a whole street lit with clay lamps on Deepavali.

Namma Chennai ("our Chennai") is our attempt to put that culture back on the map. We took about two kilometres of the old city around Chennai Central, which covers Park Town, George Town, Periamet and Chintadripet, and rebuilt it in 3D from real OpenStreetMap data. Then we filled it with the things that make it Chennai: its landmarks and their history, its festivals, its homes, its kolams and its sounds.

Everything is in English and Tamil. Nothing is made up without being labelled as such. And every photograph, upload and postcard goes through Cloudinary.

Try it now: open chennai-diorama.pages.dev and press Watch the film at the bottom left for a two minute tour of everything in this guide.

The city

Real streets, built in codeஉண்மையான தெருக்கள்

2,449 building footprints, 795 road segments, 77 railway lines, the Cooum river, the Buckingham Canal and the temple tanks. All from OpenStreetMap.

  • One shader paints every building: floors, windows, rolling shop shutters, lit windows at night, and the black water tanks on every terrace.
  • Shop boards in Tamil and English suit each neighbourhood: steel vessels in George Town, lodges near Central, flowers at Pookadai.
  • Traffic drives on the left, trains pull into Central, and unmapped blocks are filled with labelled estimated houses.
Landmarks

Every landmark has a storyஒவ்வொரு இடத்திற்கும் ஒரு கதை

Twenty places, each with researched history, real archive photographs credited to their authors, and quick facts.

Places include Chennai Central, Ripon Building, Victoria Public Hall, Southern Railway HQ, Moore Market, Fort St. George, People's Park, Memorial Hall, Kandha Kottam and its tank, the Government College of Fine Arts, Chintadripet's weavers, filter kaapi and Pookadai's flower market.

Stories, deeper

Then and now, photos and essaysஅன்றும் இன்றும்

Only genuine historical photographs are used for comparisons. Drag across to see a century of change.

Meaning. Kandha Kottam is a Murugan temple on Rasappa Chetty Street in Park Town, linked to the poet saint Vallalar. Its tank, like every temple tank, once stored the neighbourhood's water.
Festivals

One city, four moodsநான்கு திருவிழாக்கள்

The switcher at the top changes the whole city: its light, decorations, doorsteps and sound.

Margazhi (mid December to mid January) is the month of the biggest kolams, drawn before sunrise. Thai Pongal is the harvest festival: new rice boiled until it overflows, a sign of plenty. Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and lamps at every door.
Festivals

Deepavali nightதீபாவளி இரவு

Fireworks are real particle systems with several burst types: peony, ring, willow and crossette. Flower-pot crackers and ground spinners go off at doorsteps. Paper star lanterns hang in windows. The festival decorations spread outward from wherever you are looking, so the change feels like the city lighting up around you.

Time and weather

From dawn to a monsoon stormநேரமும் வானிலையும்

Rain is drawn as a screen-space effect at three depths, because at this scale a real raindrop would be smaller than a pixel. Thunder arrives a moment after each flash, like it does in real life.

Underground

The metro under your feetநகரின் கீழ் ஒரு நகரம்

  • Blue Line: Airport to Wimco Nagar. Green Line: Central to St Thomas Mount. Both meet at Central Metro.
  • Real street entrances with bilingual signs: S3, S4 and B5 near Central, A2 and A3 at High Court.
  • Four-car trains run through the tunnels.
  • Phase 2 lines (including the Red Line) are being built elsewhere and don't pass through here, so they are honestly left out.
Step inside

Kandha Kottam at duskஅந்தியில் கந்த கோட்டம்

Rajagopuram is the gateway tower. Deepasthambam is the lamp pillar, kodimaram the gilded flagstaff, mayil Murugan's peacock. The vimanam rises over the sanctum. The deity is deliberately not shown, out of respect.
Step inside

Festival homesபண்டிகை வீடுகள்

The four days of Pongal: Bhogi (clearing out the old), Thai Pongal (boiling new rice), Mattu Pongal (honouring cattle), Kaanum Pongal (visiting family; in Chennai, the Marina fills up). These homes are illustrative composites, not real families.
Kolam Studio

Draw a kolam, the real wayகோலம் வரையுங்கள்

The maths. Sikku kolams loop around a grid of dots. We model them as mirror curves: the line travels diagonally and bounces off mirrors placed between dots. A search finds mirror layouts that turn the whole pattern into one unbroken line, the traditional kambi form.

How Cloudinary powers it

There's no backend. The browser talks to Cloudinary directly, using only a cloud name and an unsigned upload preset, so no secret is ever exposed.

FeatureCloudinary capabilityHow we use it
Archive photosFetch delivery, f_auto, q_auto, g_auto80+ Wikimedia photos sized for pins, thumbnails, headers and full screen, cropped around the subject.
Responsive imagesWidth transforms in srcsetPhones download small files automatically.
Visitor memoriesUnsigned upload API, tags, contextPhotos, video and audio tagged by place, category and festival, with caption and display name.
Shared mapClient-side resource listEveryone's memories appear as pins, with no database.
AI auto-taggingAuto-tagging add-onTags like "oil lamp" or "cattle" sort memories into Deepavali, Pongal or Margazhi.
Kolam postcardsText layers, c_pad, a_, bo_One upload becomes a bilingual postcard with a postmark and border.
Shared kolamsUpload with context metadataPattern data travels with the image so others see it on a doorstep.
VideoVideo deliveryAuto format and quality, generated poster frames.
Share cardCrop, e_gradient_fade, text layersThe link preview image is a Cloudinary render with Tamil and English titles.
FallbackStartup probeIf fetch is restricted, photos load from Wikimedia and nothing breaks.
Lessons learned: Hind Madurai renders Tamil correctly in text layers; commas and slashes in overlay text need double escaping (%252C); unsigned uploads need auto-tagging enabled on the preset itself.

Honesty

What's real and what isn'tஉண்மையும் கற்பனையும்

Cultural work should be honest about where it comes from. Every label in the app follows these rules.

CategoryWhat it covers
RealStreet layout, building footprints, railway, river, canal, temple tanks, metro tunnels and entrances, building, shop and street names (OpenStreetMap, November 2026).
EstimatedBuilding heights where OSM has none. Infill houses in unmapped blocks (labelled on hover).
StylisedEvery landmark. Each follows the real building's character, not its measurements.
IllustrativeThe three family homes. Composites, badged in the app. No real family or address.
Left out on purposeThe deity in the sanctum. A proposed high-speed rail station. Unbuilt metro lines.
GeneratedSound is synthesised. "Traditional form" kolams use a traditional grid with a computer-found route; "Original design" means we drew it.
PrivacyMemories attach only to public places, never homes. The form asks people not to share addresses or faces without consent.
